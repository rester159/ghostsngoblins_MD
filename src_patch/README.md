# GnG features patch layer

The port has no source, so new features are freestanding 68000 C/asm linked at
`$380000` (the padding above the original 3.3 MiB image) and hooked into the engine
by `tools/patch_gng.py --features src_patch/build`.

```bash
make -C src_patch
python3 tools/patch_gng.py "Ghosts'n Goblins (SMD) (PD).bin" build/GhostsNGoblins_plus.bin --features src_patch/build
```

Without `--features` the patcher produces the compatibility-only ROM.

## Features so far

- Boot flow: on a cold boot the SEGA and CAPCOM intros, then **ARCADE / HOME**, with `rester159, 2026` at the bottom. The previous port's own splash ("ARCADE MACHINE" and "PROGRAMED BY FRANK", `$CA6`) is never shown.
- **Intros** (`intro.c`, as in the Black Tiger port): the Revenge of Shinobi SEGA logo with the Sonic 1 "SE-GA" chant, then the SF2' CAPCOM logo with its FM jingle, about 9.5 s; Start skips them. The chant is streamed to the YM2612 DAC at 16 kHz by a small Z80 loop from a 32 KiB ROM bank; the jingle is written by the 68k. Afterwards the game's Z80 program (136 bytes from `$202380`, normally loaded only at power-on) is reloaded and its sound init re-run. On PAL every fifth frame steps twice, so the timing stays at 60 Hz. The logo art, palettes, chant and jingle are Sega's and Capcom's and aren't in this repository: `mkintro.py` reads them from the local intro kits (`SEGA_KIT`, `CHANT_KIT`, `CAPCOM_KIT`) into `build/intro.inc`, and without the kits the build skips the intros. That data links in its own section (`.intro`, `build/intro.bin`) at `$350000`, the padding past the original image, so the code blob at `$380000` stays small.
- **ARCADE**: the original title and attract loop. C inserts coins, Start starts, A takes over C's old action.
- **HOME**: 1 PLAYER starts straight into the intro. Start pauses and resumes during play, and music keeps running.
- **ARCADE → DIP SWITCHES**:
  - Coinage: 1C/1P, 1C/2P, 1C/3P, 2C/1P, 3C/1P, free play.
  - Lives: 3, 4, 5, 7.
  - Bonus life: 20K 70K every 70K, 30K 80K every 80K, 20K 80K, 30K 80K.
  - Difficulty: the arcade's four settings, NORMAL (`$06`, what the port shipped), EASY (`$00`), DIFFICULT (`$18`), VERY DIFFICULT (`$20`): the starting rank in `$FFC501`.
  - Demo sound, continue.
- **HOME → OPTIONS**:
  - Lives 1–9; continues 0–9 or infinite (Start continues during the countdown).
  - Bonus life, including none; difficulty.
  - English / Japanese title (arcade Makaimura logo on the menus, title screen and attract overlay).
  - Sound test.
- **High scores**: separate top-10 tables for Arcade and Home. Initials entry at game over, and TOP SCORE starts from the saved best.
- **Developer** (Home screen: Up Down Up Down Left Right Left Right, until a hardware reset):
  - Stage 1–6, starting lives, infinite lives, difficulty, sound test.
  - Launched games are practice runs and aren't ranked.
- DIP switches, Home options, the title choice and both tables are saved to battery SRAM.
- **HOME → 2 PLAYERS**: Maximo on pad 2, running on Arthur's own player routines through a context swap (`p2.c`).
  - **Movement:** the same walking, jumping, landing (terrain and tombstones), animation timing and facing as Arthur.
  - **Weapons:** his lances spawn from his own position (`$80BA`) into the shared weapon slots. The game moves them and scores their hits; both players share the engine's few on-screen shots.
  - **Getting hit:** enemies hit him through the engine's own collision, with Arthur's knockback and armor shatter (`$55E0`, run for him), down to boxers, then death.
  - **Death and respawn:** after dying, a player respawns 3 s later next to their partner (if the partner is alive and on the ground), otherwise where they fell. They come back armored (`$7FA4`, `$6456`, `$6928`, armor set `$FFC328 = 0`) with 3 s of the game's own blink invincibility (`$FFC309`).
  - **Arthur in co-op:** while Maximo is in the game, Arthur's death doesn't restart the stage (hook at `$61DC`).
  - **Lives and credits:** each player has their own lives (the Lives option). An out-of-lives player uses a shared credit to rejoin; with none left they are out. If Maximo is out, Arthur follows the normal 1P rules. When both are out, the game's continue and game-over flow runs.
  - **Each other:** the players pass through each other, but a player falling onto the partner's head is set on it and bounces off automatically with a full jump, once per jump; a second landing on the head falls through, and touching the ground re-arms it (`head_bounce` in `p2.c`).
  - **HUD:** "PLAYER2" and Maximo's lives in the top-right, in the game's own HUD tiles.
  - **Developer menu:** PLAYERS 1/2 launches co-op into any stage. Stages 1–6 were each run for 3,000 frames of random co-op play with no faults.
  - **Score:** shared team score; Maximo's kills count towards the single score.
- **HOME → FIREBRAND'S QUEST**: a one-player campaign as the Red Arremer (`firebrand.c`).
  - **Art:** Firebrand's own frames from Demon's Crest (stand, 6-frame walk, fire, jump, 6-frame flight, landing), captured headlessly with `tools/headless/snes_capture.py` (snes9x core) into `art/firebrand/dc/`, mapped to sprite palette line 0 by hand (`art/firebrand/src/dc.py`) and scaled to 70% so he matches Arthur's size (`art/firebrand/src/build.py`: palette-aware downscale; feet or body centre as the anchor). His head is a hand-drawn stamp (`art/firebrand/src/head.py`) placed where template matching finds his head in each source frame, because the dithered original turns to noise at 70%; the firing pose keeps its own head. Every frame Arthur can show maps to a pose of the same meaning and facing; walking and hovering play Firebrand's 6-frame cycles instead of Arthur's (`firebrand.c`). Frames wider than 32 px add two 8×32 columns at tiles `$690`/`$694`. His death is the game's enemy burn-up flames (palette line 2). The bank (108 × `0x300`) is at `$3A0000`.
  - **Hover:** hold jump after the top of a jump to hold the altitude for up to 2 s (`$FF9404` zeroed before the engine's fall step); landing refills it. Left/right steers at walking speed through the engine's wall-checked walk moves (`$11469C`/`$1146CE`). His flight cycle plays while hovering.
  - **One attack:** the lance's mechanics, drawn as the fireball from `art/firebrand/src/fire.py` (uploaded in place of the lance art for each shot slot, `$11711E`; right-moving shots get their two pieces swapped so the picture faces forward, and left-moving ones are drawn 14 px ahead because the game starts them over his body; either way the fireball leaves the mouth of his firing pose). The HUD weapon box shows the game's own fire icon (`$1037D4`). Pickups never change it.
  - **One hit:** never armored (`$FFC328 = 1`), so the first hit kills.
  - **Boxes:** weapon and armor pickups give 1,000 points instead (the game's score message `$0C00|8` on the queue at `$FFB400`).
  - **Head:** a hand-drawn head is stamped on every frame where his head is in profile (see Art); the firing pose gets an open-mouth version with fire in the mouth.
  - **Stage map and stage end:** the map marker between lives is Firebrand's head (`hook_map_marker` at `$113DA2` points it at tiles `$69C-$69F`), and the stage-end sequence's frames (`$C0000-$C1600`, the `$8AAD4` table) map to his poses, the fist-raise to his wings-spread flight frame.
  - **Frog:** the frog frames are in the ROM, but no code path in this port that I could find selects them; a frog frame would be shown unchanged.
  - **Start:** skips the princess scene (`$E0C`) and goes straight into stage 1.
  - **Developer menu:** PLAYERS 1 / 2 / FIREBRAND.
- **Engine speed (all modes, features build only):**
  - Measured with `tools/re/probe_rom.py` (timestamps every stage-1 update and every DMA) and `tools/re/uc_profile.py` (Unicorn, estimated cycles per routine). In play, with deaths excluded, 1P runs at 0.97-1.0 updates per frame in every stage; the earlier ~0.69 figure counted death and map sequences.
  - The sound driver's request scan (`$11CAB8`) tested 512 bits one at a time every update; it now skips clear longs and bytes (`sound_scan`, checked against the original by `tools/re/test_sound_scan.py`: 300 random cases, identical Z80 commands).
  - The object/player pass (`$69A2`) mapped object types through a 14-compare chain; it is now a table (`src_patch/inplace/obj_type.s`, checked by `tools/re/test_inplace.py`: identical RAM and VDP writes over whole updates).
  - Firebrand's shot fix-up only scans the sprite table while shots are out.
  - The stage 1-2 sprite build's type chain (`$B2B4`, `inplace/spr12.s`) is a table as well (identical RAM and VDP writes over 80 co-op updates, `test_ab.py`).
  - The other per-slot type chains are tables too: the 40-slot passes at `$114704` (stages 2-6, `inplace/obj_sum.s`) and `$768A` (stage 1, `slot_dispatch`), and the handler pick at `$6A88` (`obj_dispatch`). Each target is entered with the same registers as before.
  - The stage-1 tombstone check (`$10442A`, run three times an update in co-op over 12 tombstones) hoists its loop-invariant tests and skips tombstones outside the player's X window with one compare (`tomb_check`; `tools/re/test_tomb.py`: 22,500 random placements, identical RAM and registers).
  - Co-op: Maximo's context swap is generated straight-line movem code over the ranges his update actually touches (`mkswap.py`, ranges traced by `tools/re/ctx_trace.py`), and his control block is one assembly sequence (`p2_control`) instead of one C call per routine.
  - The sprite buffer clear before every sprite build (128 single `clr.l`) is a movem clear (`clear_sat`).
  - The dragon and the Satans (stage 6 starts with all nine dragon nodes and both Satans alive, mostly asleep): measured in Unicorn over stage 6 co-op updates (`uc_profile.py`'s cycle model, with the cost of each object-pass slot attributed by type), the dragon cost 30.6 lines an update and the Satans 9.9, out of ~247. Most of it was the engine around the C: each segment went through the weapon test's set-up and call (`$1057A0`, `$BE3A`, ~670 cycles, also when it isn't on screen and can't be hit) and a C call for a frozen segment (~450 cycles). Now:
    - `dragon_wtest` (hook `$114D58`): for dragon nodes and Satans that aren't both live and on screen, the weapon test's result (its hit list cleared, nothing hit) without the table set-up and call; same RAM and registers. Other types go on to `$1057A0` as before.
    - `dragon_entry` handles the body segments in state 1 itself (frozen: only the far-off-screen check; awake: the passed-on direction, the 2 px move on the C table `dragon_vel`, the check); the head and every other state still go to `dragon_update`.
    - `satan_update` does the asleep state (`$BF1E`) inline instead of building the inputs and calling `satan_step`.
    - Result: dragon 19.7 lines, Satans 7.0 (asleep); awake segments 24.0 -> 18.1 (stage 6) and 32.9 -> 20.7 (stage 4). Stage 6 co-op: 0.785 -> 0.871 updates per frame (10 seeds; 3 seeds 0.783 -> 0.881); other stages and modes unchanged within noise.
    - Checked against the previous build in Unicorn from the same RAM images with the same random input on both pads (Arthur kept invincible): the whole of RAM (`$FF0000-$FFFBFF`) and every VDP write identical after every update, over 1,500-6,000 updates from each of 40 starting points in stages 1-6 (co-op, 1P and Firebrand; the dragon asleep, woken, flying on screen and shot down in stages 3, 4 and 6; the Satans asleep, woken and firing in stages 5 and 6). `tools/re/test_satan.py`: 0 mismatches (32,000 handler calls).
  - The same weapon-test early-out for every enemy (`dragon_wtest`, `$114D58`, all types but 8): in stages 3-6 the object pass (`$1055D4`) runs the weapon test (`$1057A0`, `$BE3A`, ~670 cycles) every other update for every live slot, and for a slot that isn't both live and on screen it can find nothing: `$BE3A` returns before it looks at any weapon (so the weapons' own on-screen flag, +9, never matters there), clearing its 16-byte hit list. The hook gives that result directly (the hit list cleared, d0/d1/d6 = 0, d2 as the table index, the dragon's flash byte as before). Type 8's test is its own (`$7201A`: it records the slot in RAM and runs `$71500`), so type 8, and every slot that is live and on screen, still goes to `$1057A0`. Stages 1 and 2 use another pass (`$69A2`) whose test already skips off-screen slots (`$6B16`), so they're unchanged.
    - Measured (Unicorn, 100 updates from co-op/1P/Firebrand snapshots): 3-6 lines an update fewer in stages 3-6 (stage 4 co-op 213 -> 207, stage 5 co-op 194 -> 189, stage 4 1P 179 -> 173, stage 5 Firebrand 170 -> 164).
    - Checked against the build before it in Unicorn (the same random input on both pads, the VBlank counter advanced once per update so the object pass alternates between its weapon and shot halves): the whole of RAM and every VDP write identical after every update, 1,500 updates from each of 147 starting points (5 points of each of: stages 1-6 in co-op, 1P and Firebrand; the dragon in stages 3 and 4 asleep, woken and shot down; the Satans of stages 5 and 6 awake, firing and killed, with the boss-end freeze; the final room with Astaroth in all three modes).
  - **Render skip** (`src_patch/pace.c`, `hook_sat_dma`, `render_gate*`): the game logic runs every update; in busy stretches the sprite layer is drawn less often, like a frame skip.
    - At the start of each update (`$6938`), `pace_start` reads where in VBlank the update starts. From NTSC line 229, or when a whole frame was lost, it skips this update's sprite build (15-20% of an update in every stage).
    - From line 241 the uploads would run into the active display (DMA ~10x slower, CPU halted), so that frame uploads nothing for the sprites (sprite table, Arthur's and Maximo's frames). The screen keeps the previous sprites; a held Arthur frame goes up with the next upload.
    - At most 3 frames in a row are held (`FS_MAX` in pace.c), so sprites are drawn at least every fourth frame. The background keeps scrolling on held frames, so sprites can lag it by a pixel or two for those frames. PAL (longer VBlank) only skips after a lost frame.
    - Everything the builds write is render state (sprite buffer and counts, enemy/shot art requests, piece counts). `tools/re/test_ab.py` with a forced skip on every other update (`-pb <fs_force>=1`) gives identical game RAM over 150 updates from co-op snapshots of all six stages.
  - Every change is checked against the previous build by `tools/re/test_ab.py`: whole updates from co-op RAM snapshots of all six stages with random input on both pads give identical RAM and VDP writes (for the render skip: with skipping off; and with it forced, identical game RAM).

- Menu text uses the arcade font (ASCII `$20–$7F` from `rom/gg1.bin`).
- **Tombstones** (all modes): the arcade's rules, translated from its check at `$9FAE` (gg3) and its box table at `$A052` (type 1: x ±12, y ±19, top at y + 19). The port had its own: landing within 20 px on a top at +20, and a side stop at 13 px that also worked in mid-air, so no jump could clear a tombstone. In the arcade a tombstone only blocks walking (probe x ± 1, y + 1); in the air it can only land you on it (probe x, y − 1, falling, inside the box: snapped to the top). Checked against the arcade (`tools/arcade/gng.py`) with the same runs: side stop at x 244 for the tombstone at 256 in both, a running jump from against it clears it in both, top at y 81 in both, and the clear/land/block takeoff ranges agree to within the 0.35 px the two start apart (see `docs/ACCURACY.md`).
- **Input timing** (all modes, `arcade_input` in `pad.c`): as in the arcade, left/right count from their second frame (this frame AND last), and a jump from the ground takes off two frames after the press (a walk frame, a standing frame, then the jump) along the direction latched at the press. See `docs/ACCURACY.md`.
- **The Satans** (stages 5 and 6, all modes; `satan.c`): the arcade's Satan (type 6, handler `$BED5`) translated routine by routine in place of the port's (`$1105C6`), which drifted left/right on directions and timers from the rank byte `$FFA208`, never flew up or down and so couldn't be reached in stage 5. Now, as the arcade: asleep until on screen (`$BF1E`); hovering with a slow drift (a random direction every 128 frames), shots every 40-50 frames while the wings are open, the wings closed for 30 frames (weapons blocked: the port's weapon code already skips `+4` = `$81`), backing off when Arthur is within 48 px, swoops at Arthur in a sine arc at 3-3.4 px/frame by rank (aimed at his height ±16-32, a level run then a 96 px rise when level with him, a straight 3.5 px/frame dash when below him), and a 3-4 px/frame dash back when off the screen; 10 hit points; the arcade's 22-frame burst before its death's score phase (`hook_satan_die`; drawn since Fixed 60). Each Satan keeps an image of the arcade's 48-byte slot in patch RAM (4 records), so the C reads like the 6809 code; the port slot gets x/y, flags, state and frame (1/2 flying, 3/4 wings closed). The shots go into the port's shot pool (`$FFC100`, free list `$FFC220` = the arcade's `<$A7/<$A8`) as its type 6, whose motion is the arcade's type `$0B` (velocity table `$29238` = `$5DBF`; `$7442` = `$8FE4`, identical for all 65,536 inputs). Checked against the arcade: `tools/re/test_satan.py` runs the same C on every recorded handler call (all states, 0 mismatches) and the ROM runs frame-locked with the arcade (see `docs/ACCURACY.md`).
- **The arcade's second random source** (`arcrand.c`, `arc_rand` / `arc_rand_asm`): the shift register at `$68F9` on the port's own copy of its state (`$FFC62C` = `$6E/$6F`, `$FFC62E` = `$70`), now seeded only at boot and game start as in the arcade; the ten stage inits reloaded `$87CD` at every stage and life start.
- **Arcade rules** (all modes unless stated; details and evidence in `docs/ACCURACY.md`): the random source runs on like the arcade's frame counter instead of restarting every life (zombie patterns no longer repeat), the item-drop cycle carries over, DIFFICULTY uses the arcade's four starting ranks (NORMAL/EASY/DIFFICULT/VERY DIFFICULT), a continue resets the score and bonus thresholds, the countdown is 640 frames and in Arcade mode takes Start with fire or jump, bonus lives stop at 1,000,000 (and aren't capped at 9), the continue countdown runs 10 to 01 on its own line, a kill scores in the update of the hit, a bonus life comes on the arcade's check frame (the ending's in stage 1's play), stage 4's fire pillars change size on the frame of their step, a one-frame fire or jump press is never lost when an update runs long, the camera doesn't climb while Arthur is in the air, a restart keeps Arthur's sub-pixel position, the random counter steps once per update like the arcade's, the terrain under the screen is the arcade's, a new game starts at 0 points (the port gave 600), the cross cancels enemy shots (and other weapons are used up on the shot types the arcade's are), the man-eater plant's hit box is the arcade's, Arthur's weapons, the zombies coming out of the ground and sinking, and the final room's flames are drawn where the arcade draws them (Firebrand's fireball, which shares the weapon builder, sits 8 px higher than before), walking off a tombstone drops straight away as in the arcade, a stage 4 enemy can no longer halt the game with fault code $8C, the checkpoint no longer restarts the music, and after the second loop the game plays on from stage 1.
- **The dragon** (stages 3, 4 and 6, all modes; `dragon.c`, `dragon_glue.s`): the arcade's (gg3 `$D518`-`$DAD8`, tables in gg4 `$5182`-`$52FD`) in place of the port's own type-12 handler, which homed on Arthur, never re-aimed (its timer byte `+$23` overlapped a counter word at `+$22`) and so flew off in a straight line, with the body replaying the head's path.
  - **Body:** head, 7 segments and the tail, one slot each, built to the head's right (+20, then +14 each, the tail +12). Every node moves on its own at 2 px/frame in one of 16 directions (`$A427`, table `$51B6`); a new direction goes from the head to the first segment after 10 frames, then on down the chain after 7 frames each (6 to the tail) (`$D955`).
  - **Head:** first the uncoiling script (`$D789`: six 10-frame steps, the head stepping once per step), then frozen until it is on screen, Arthur is no more than 32 px below it and 2-5 ticks of 16 frames have passed (`$D797`-`$D7C0`). Then it follows its (frames, direction) scripts (`$5216`, `$52C2`, `$52E0`), lunges at Arthur when he's more than 24 px ahead of it (aim `$8FE4`, by a random against a rank threshold), turns round at the arena's end or 94 px past Arthur, and climbs or dives toward his height (`$D818`; stage 6 steers by the height it started at instead, `$D90A`). Every 128 frames it opens its mouth and roars (sound `$21`).
  - **Fire** (`$D981`): when Arthur is within 72 px of its height, on the 32nd frame and one in four by the random, if a projectile slot is free: a fireball from its mouth that flies straight along its side at 3.125 px/frame (the port's type 9 aimed at Arthur at 2.26 px/frame: its aim at `$107510` is gone and its speed table at `$107540` is `dragon_fire_vel`).
  - **Hits** (`$DA15`): every node can be hit (the object pass skipped all but the head, `$105798`); a hit segment survives and the dragon loses its tail instead, 200 points each; six hits kill the head, and every segment goes with it. The weapon box is the arcade's ±12 x ±10 (port ±16 x ±16), the score index 4 (200; the port gave 1,000), and weapon 2 passes through (the weapon-hit table `$2AC94`, kind 11). The head burns 21 frames, shows "1000" for 63 and then gives the 1,000 points (`$0C09`), and its removal starts the stage end (the port's boss count, `$10EA0C`; stage 6's dragon gives no key). Segments show "200" for 63 frames after 36 of burning. The burn-up flames themselves aren't drawn: the type-12 sprite table has no flame frames.
  - **Spawn** (`$106F10`): stages 3 and 4 at the camera + (`$180`, `$40`) of the spawn frame, set up the frame after, as the arcade's; stage 6 in the tower at the absolute (7824, 2016) (`$EB4B`, the record's absolute branch `$EB5B`), where the port used the camera offsets too.
  - The arcade's second random source (`$68F9`, `arc_rand` in `arcrand.c`, shared with the Satans) drives the lunges and the fire; in the arcade 34 places in the enemy code step it, here the dragon, the Satans and the port's own copies, so its choices follow the arcade's logic but not always its sequence.
  - Checked against the arcade frame by frame (`tools/arcade/gng.py` in lockstep with the port, Arthur's position, the frame counter and the shift register copied over from the arcade, the stage 3 Red Arremers parked off screen): every node's position, direction, state and HP, the fire and the score are identical in stage 3 (3,000 frames, shot down segment by segment to the key; the stage-end flag on the same frame, 1604) and stage 4 (4,000 frames, the same, frame 1572), and in stage 6 over 4,000 frames of the dragon waking and flying round Arthur in the tower, plus its death (+1,000, no stage end). The only differences are the head's position on its spawn frame, before it is set up, and fireballs leaving the screen a frame apart (the arcade's fireball fraction is leftover RAM).
- **Arthur's frame timing and the stage 1 boss** (all modes, `arthur.s`; evidence in `docs/ACCURACY.md`, Fixed 23-29): the stand-up frame after a crouch doesn't walk and the crouch-entry frame does, as in the arcade (also for Maximo, whose control block now uses the walk gate's answer); stepping off the bottom of a ladder, and the first ground step of a stage start, check the floor at Arthur's own y only, so he falls the 2 px a frame later as the arcade's does instead of snapping down; the armor flag `$FFC328` is set on the hit frame, not when the knockback lands; the rank creeps up with play time (`rank_creep`, +`$6B`/256 to the working rank every 256 frames of play, reset at each start; a stage clear adds 4 to the base rank); the Unicorn's walk doesn't follow the floor; stage 1's terrain map ring holds the arcade's columns (cam/16 - 15 .. + 16). With these the Unicorn fight runs update for update as the arcade's.
- **After a boss, weapons off the screen, the spawner and the terrain windows** (all modes, `stageend.s`, `mapwin.s`; evidence in `docs/ACCURACY.md`, Fixed 34-37):
  - **Stage end:** once a boss is dead (`$FFC8D6 = 2`, stages 1-6), as in the arcade's stage-end frame (`$72C9`) the enemies, their shots, Arthur's weapons and the two item slots stop where they are and aren't drawn (their flags are cleared for each sprite build and put back at the start of the next update), nothing hits anything and nothing spawns. Arthur can still throw into a free weapon slot, and weapon slot 0 counts as taken (the arcade's key is in it), so he gets one more lance at most, which stays where it was thrown. The 16x16 sprite that stayed next to Arthur while the key fell was the item in the first item slot (`$FF9780`). The key, the platforms and the stage-end sequence run as before.
  - **Weapons off the screen:** a weapon's +9 flag is the arcade's on-screen flag, set before it moves from the camera and the weapon's window (`w_visible`); off the screen it flies on to the end of its range but isn't drawn and hits nothing (every hit test already needed +9).
  - **Spawner:** called after the scroll in every stage loop, as the arcade's (stage 1's already was), so camera-triggered spawns come on the arcade's update and a new enemy first moves the update after.
  - **Terrain:** the collision-map windows of stages 2-5 start at cam/16 - 15, the arcade's, like stage 1's.
- **Music volume** (all modes): the music is 6 dB quieter; the sound effects are unchanged. The music (stage tunes, map and intro themes, jingles) is the sequencer's recorded YM2612 register streams, and the action effects are separate PCM clips, so the patcher adds 8 steps of attenuation to each carrier operator's total level in the stream data (`attenuate_music` and `MUSIC_ATT` in `tools/patch_gng.py`). Engine variables used: `$FFC000` score (BCD), `$FFC004` TOP SCORE, `$FFC500` lives,
`$FFC501` difficulty rank, `$FFC561` rank floor, `$FFC512` stage index (0-5 = stages 1-6, 6 = the final room),
`$FFC570` play state (2 = continue countdown), `$FF7FE8` credits. A debug launch to a later
stage uses the game's own stage advance (`$FFC512 = n`, `jmp $113B70`) on the first frame of play.
Entering a stage's code directly from the start path crashes, because it expects that loader.

## Memory

- `$FF5000–$FF50FF`: `Persist` (`common.h`), which survives the game's soft restarts. Validated by the magic `MXMP`.
- `$FF5100–$FF5EFF`: C `.bss` (no `.data`; everything is cleared at boot). `arthur.s`, then `satan.c` and `arcrand.c` (the 4 × 52-byte Satan records), are linked last, so their variables come after everyone else's and the earlier addresses (`menu_frames` at `$FF5137`, ...) stay put.
- `stageend.s` is linked after `arthur.s`, `satan.c` and `arcrand.c`; its 51 bytes (`se_save`, `se_hidden`, `se_slot0`) come last.
- `petite.c`, `petite_glue.s`, `skull.c` and `skull_glue.s` are linked after everything else: the devil and skull records (6 and 8 × 52 bytes), `pd_glob` (the arcade's `$1574`-`$1579`) and the glue's saved registers come after all earlier variables.
- `bossart.c` and `bossart_glue.s` are linked last (`$FF590C`-`$FF5987`: the star tags, stage 2's two window records, the Satans' pose buffers' state); `.bss` ends at `$FF5988`.
- `opening.c` is linked last (`$FF5A50`-`$FF5AE6`: the opening's load state, the top score's digits, the stage-start hiding's saved CRAM); `.bss` ends at `$FF5AE7`.
- Arthur's timing flags use two bytes of the player control block that the game never touches (`$FF9451`, `$FF9455`) and the knockback marker one of the physics block (`$FFC343`); both blocks are in Maximo's context swap and are cleared by the game's own inits (`$7FA4`, `$6456`).
- `$FF5F00`: the SRAM transfer routine, copied there at run time.
- `$FF2BE1–$FF6FFF` is never touched by the game. Checked over 24,000 frames of boot, attract and stage 1 play plus 6,000 frames of play in each of stages 2–6 (via the developer launch), and no instruction addresses it directly.

## SRAM

The header declares 8 KiB at `$200001–$203FFF` (odd bytes). Because the ROM is larger than
2 MiB, SRAM is switched in with `$A130F1 = 1` only for the length of a transfer. `sram_xfer`
copies itself to work RAM and runs there with interrupts off, so no code ever executes from
the switched window. The save format (`sram.c`) is magic `GNGS`, a version, the options and a checksum.
